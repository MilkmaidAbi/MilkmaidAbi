### Linux on my Mac

**MSL** is this thing my counterpart made because apparently macOS wasn't enough and he desperately needed Linux on it too.

It runs actual Linux distributions using Apple's Virtualization framework, opens Linux applications as native Mac windows, and has its own X11 server written in Swift.

Which is admittedly kind of neat.

I would've done it differently. Probably. I haven't actually thought about how.

Anyway, it works, and there's an absurd amount of documentation because I dunno when to stop writing. OwO

### Terminal things

**ytcui** is a terminal YouTube client.

It has ncurses, mpv integration, playlists, coloured thumbnails, vim keybindings, and eighteen colour themes.

### OneInstallSystem

**OIS** is a POSIX shell installer and updater.

It's supposed to make installing software easier, which is funny because developing it seems to have been considerably harder than just writing an installer normally.

It handles updates, uninstallation, rollbacks, checksums, and thirteen package managers.

I don't really have anything sarcastic to say about this one.

It's useful.

Unfortunately.

## How I Work

### Parallel development

I don't really do things in order.

I'll start working on one feature, get distracted by another, make three different implementations, forget why I started, and eventually combine whichever ones aren't completely broken.

It's kind of like selective breeding.

Except instead of plants, it's code.

And instead of carefully selecting desirable traits, I'm mostly just hoping something survives.

It works surprisingly often. 

Don't look at my commit history - I regularly accidentally rm -rf .git into oblivion.

### Documentation-heavy

I write a lot of documentation.

A LOT.

Sometimes more than the actual code.

I like things being properly explained, okay? I don't want to come back six months later and wonder what sort of idiot wrote this.

Even though I know exactly which idiot wrote it. ME!

I have standards.

They're just inconsistently enforced.

Also, I like my READMEs looking cute and all.

### On AI

Yeah, I use AI.

Quite a bit, actually.

I use it to learn things, understand unfamiliar code, work through problems, and occasionally untangle whatever horrible mess I've managed to create.

I'm not going to pretend I wrote every single line of everything myself.

That's just dishonest.

The ideas are mine, the decisions are mine, and I still have to understand what I'm putting into my projects.

I don't particularly care if someone thinks that makes me less of a programmer.

Well, I care a little.
